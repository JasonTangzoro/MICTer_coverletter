Dear Editor,

We submit our manuscript, “MICTer localizes candidate critical transition windows through manifold-informed landscape dynamics”, for consideration as a research article.

Sparse single-cell time courses reveal changing populations while leaving the timing of transitions between sampled states unresolved. Our study connects continuous population reconstruction to a focused question: when does the fitted landscape reorganize, and which molecular changes and predicted fate responses accompany those intervals?

MICTer combines manifold-aware latent cell-state encoding, potential-driven dynamics and temporal landscape analysis. The shared representation links observed expression to reconstructed states and molecular readouts through a decoder. Transport matching fits population evolution, while a conditional Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman residual regularizes the potential. Temporal potential change at fixed states and dynamical activity define a score for localizing candidate transition windows.

We evaluate this framework across five time series: definitive endoderm differentiation, targeted iPSC differentiation, EMT/MET, pancreatic endocrine differentiation and human iPSC cardiac differentiation. The analyses reconstruct changing populations and branch-associated landscapes, then connect selected windows to gene expression, potential sensitivity and predicted perturbation responses. In pancreatic differentiation, the primary window spans days 2.78–3.03 and accompanies β-cell and enterochromaffin-like fate tendencies. In cardiac differentiation, days 2.62–3.20 precede the emergence of cardiomyocyte and cardiac fibroblast programmes.

Perturbation comparisons show how gene identity and intervention timing shape predicted lineage composition. Pancreatic responses were generally stronger for day-3 than day-1 initiation, while cardiac responses to selected genes were attenuated at day 5 relative to day 3. The targeted iPSC and EMT/MET analyses provide additional comparisons of stage-dependent responses. These results identify gene–stage combinations for experimental investigation.

The study provides a common route from cell-state geometry to transition timing and molecular hypotheses across differentiation systems. We believe it will interest researchers in computational systems biology, developmental dynamics and single-cell analysis.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
